# eBook Covers That Feel Designed + Finished-Book / Share Experience

Scope: practical, codifiable guidance for (a) a procedural, theme-aware cover generator (SVG/CSS, type pairings, palettes) and (b) the finished-book and share experience in a desktop, no-backend AI eBook creator for non-designers. Source quality caveat: most cover-design guidance comes from practitioner, vendor, or self-publishing service blogs (Reedsy, IngramSpark, Written Word Media, Self-Publishing School, The Book Designer). Treat their font lists and conventions as consensus craft opinion, not empirical research. Primary or official sources used where available: Amazon KDP help, Gumroad help, NYPL Labs author's write-up, Butterick's Practical Typography.

## 1. Cover design: genre conventions, thumbnail legibility, template-y vs designed, layout variation

### Takeaway
Covers signal genre "at a glance" through four levers: typeface character, color/mood, imagery type (photo, illustration, object, pattern, type-only), and amount of white space. Across genres, sources converge on the same rules: one dominant title, at most 2 font families, high text/background contrast, one focal element instead of a collage, generous white space, and a title readable at about 150–200 px tall. Professional variety comes from choosing among a small set of compositional archetypes (typographic, minimalist, object, pattern, illustrated, and so on) per genre, not from swapping text into one template.

### Cited Findings

**Genre signaling (principle)**
- A cover should communicate genre at a glance. Color, composition, typography, and art type (photo composite vs painted illustration) all shape how a book is perceived. Designers then add their own twist on the conventions. — [Reedsy: Book cover design](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design)
- Imagery, typography, and palette should make it immediately clear whether a book is a thriller, children's book, cookbook, or practical self-help guide. — [IngramSpark: What makes a book cover work](https://www.ingramspark.com/blog/whatmakesabookcoverwork?)
- Typography communicates the book's metadata (title, author, tagline) and also mood, genre, and intended audience. — [Writer's Digest: Anatomy of a book cover](https://writersdigest.com/writing-articles/the-anatomy-of-a-book-cover-a-guide-for-authors)

**Genre-by-genre typography (font examples per genre)** — all from [Self-Publishing School: Book cover typography guide](https://self-publishingschool.com/book-cover-typography-guide/)
- Self-help / personal development: clean sans (Futura, Avenir, Poppins, Open Sans, Raleway).
- Business / nonfiction: neo-grotesque or geometric sans (Helvetica Neue, Univers, Montserrat, Proxima Nova), with a serif (Minion Pro) in support.
- Memoir: mostly text serifs (Baskerville, Freight Text, Merriweather, Lora).
- Cookbook / food: slab and display serifs (Museo Slab, Clarendon, Freight Display, Playfair Display).
- Children's / middle grade: rounded, playful (Baloo, Fredoka One, Nunito, Luckiest Guy). Picture books: hand-drawn or script (Pacifico, Amatic SC, Lobster).
- Thriller / crime: bold condensed sans (Bebas Neue, Impact, Compacta).
- Romance: high-contrast display serif (Playfair Display, Didot, Abril Fatface).
- Fantasy: classical titling serifs (Cinzel, Trajan, Perpetua Titling).
- The same source gives no tech/how-to guidance.
- Use 1–3 families. Two is most common: a display face for the title plus a neutral face for subtitle and author. Each family needs a distinct role. Build hierarchy with size, weight, case, and color. Test scripts at thumbnail size. Check kerning at display sizes. — [Self-Publishing School](https://self-publishingschool.com/book-cover-typography-guide/)

**Genre color, imagery, and mood**
- Nonfiction and self-help: elegant typography leads, imagery is minimal, and off-white or near-white backgrounds are partly returning. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design)
- Literary fiction: serene or muted palettes with simple imagery. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design)
- Children's: mostly illustrated, vibrant or muted depending on style, with big stylized characters or a minimalist approach. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design)
- Thriller: darker "urban" schemes and neon blues or violets, with cinematic or abstract imagery. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design). A Reedsy genre chart (seen in a search snippet, not fetched) lists dark, high-contrast black and red, bold or condensed sans, and silhouettes. — [Reedsy: How to design a book cover](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-design/how-to-design-a-book-cover/)
- Romance: bright, flat colors with little gradient. Sci-fi/fantasy: two or three shades of color. Horror: dark and desaturated. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design)
- Self-help typically uses clean, simple type to convey clarity and authority. Sans-serifs often dominate nonfiction as a signal of modernity and professionalism. Self-help often uses warm colors and approachable type. — Search-snippet summaries of [Self-Publishing School](https://self-publishingschool.com/book-cover-typography-guide/) and [Spines: Visuals in nonfiction covers](https://spines.com/the-role-of-visuals-in-non-fiction-book-cover-design/) (snippets only, not fully verified)
- Memoirs and biographies often have clean covers with a photo (frequently childhood or iconic moments) and emphasize typography. — [Goodreads blog: Balancing genre expectations (Linnea Gradin, Reedsy)](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/22814108-balancing-genre-expectations-with-standing-out-book-covers-by-linnea-gr) (snippet)

**Cover style archetypes and their genre fit** — [The Book Designer: 10 types of book covers](https://www.thebookdesigner.com/types-of-book-covers-10-design-styles/)
- Typographic (type carries the design): self-help, mystery, lighter or literary fiction. Rare for fantasy and sci-fi.
- Minimalist (one or two key elements, limited palette): modern fiction, memoir, self-help, business.
- Retro (Art Deco, mid-century; simple shapes, rich colors such as gold and black): historical fiction, period memoir.
- Scene-setting: fantasy and sci-fi.
- Portrait: biography, memoir, character-driven fiction.
- Illustrated: children's, fantasy, romance, adventure.
- Object-based (a single symbolic object): romance and nonfiction about a specific subject.
- Pattern-based (repeating abstract graphics): art and design books, and any fiction when paired with strong typography.
- Photographic: contemporary fiction, factual books, travel. Cityscape: urban fiction, travel.
- Pick one primary emphasis rather than equal focus on everything. Stock photos can look generic.

**Thumbnail legibility and platform specs**
- KDP recommends 2,560 px tall × 1,600 px wide (1.6:1), at least 300 PPI, JPEG, RGB. The longest side must be at least 500 px. SVG is not accepted. Don't stretch images. Covers with white or very light backgrounds should get a 3–4 px medium-gray border so they don't blend into the store page. Cover text must not be "blurred, cut off, or fading into the background." Cover must include title and author, matching the metadata. No placeholder or template text. No pricing or promo text. — [Amazon KDP: Cover image guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G6GTK3T3NUHKLEFX)
- KDP itself gives no thumbnail-specific guidance. — [Amazon KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G6GTK3T3NUHKLEFX)
- Practitioner consensus: shrink the cover to about 150–200 px tall (or about 160 px wide) and check that the title is still readable. Use large, bold type and simple images that read at small sizes. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design); [Indies Unlimited: Legible titles in thumbnail](https://indiesunlimited.com/2017/11/21/the-case-for-legible-titles-book-covers-in-thumbnail/) (snippet)
- Counterpoint: some designers argue thumbnail legibility is overstated and full-size attractiveness matters more. — [Goodreads: An argument against designing for thumbnail](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/10978349-an-argument-against-designing-for-thumbnail)
- A widely repeated claim that "92% of Amazon bestsellers have thumbnail-readable titles" comes from an uncited vendor blog. Treat it as unverified (flagged in search results; not used).

**What reads as amateur or template-y vs designed** — [Written Word Media: Three big cover mistakes](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/three-big-mistakes-to-avoid-when-designing-a-book-cover/)
- Use no more than two font families. Get hierarchy from weights within a family (light, semibold, bold, italic, extended).
- Avoid title fonts that are too light or too decorative.
- The title is the largest element, then the author, then subtitle and quotes. For long titles, use a condensed width and/or break the title across lines to maximize size.
- Place text in open, low-detail areas of the image. Use light-on-dark or dark-on-light contrast. Bright colors advance, dark colors recede.
- Don't collage multiple images. Use one on-topic image. Leave breathing room.
- Pre-made templates "can look nearly identical to other books" and often don't fit longer titles.
- Colors, fonts, and imagery must all support one message.
- Leave generous white space and don't fill every inch. — [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/book-cover-design)
- Cluttered composition and missing genre signals are cited as common amateur errors. — [Written Word Media: 5 most common mistakes](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2019/02/07/the-5-most-common-mistakes-in-book-cover-design-and-how-to-avoid-them/) (snippet)

**How professionals systematize variation (series systems)**
- Penguin's 1960s Marber grid divided the cover into fixed zones for the publisher logo, title/author, and image. It used Akzidenz Grotesk, maximized space for artwork, and was adapted across series. — [Romek Marber: Penguin grid](https://romekmarber.com/portfolio/penguin-grid); [Design Museum: Penguin Books](https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books)
- Coralie Bickford-Smith's Penguin clothbound classics use patterns that "all conform to the same grid." Each title gets a thematic motif (an object from the book), repeated as a pattern. Some later covers distort the grid for effect. — [Penguin: Coralie Bickford-Smith, Penguin English Library](https://www.penguin.co.uk/discover/articles/coralie-bickford-smith-penguin-english-library) (via search snippet)

### Inferences
- **Codifiable genre-to-style map** (synthesized from Reedsy, Self-Publishing School, and The Book Designer). Each genre gets a title-font class, a palette mood, and 2–3 allowed composition archetypes:
  - Business/self-help: geometric or grotesque sans, bold. Off-white or one saturated accent. Typographic, minimalist, or object archetype.
  - How-to/tech: no source found. Extrapolating from business/nonfiction: grotesque sans, plus a monospaced or diagram motif.
  - Memoir: text serif, mixed case. Muted palette. Portrait-placeholder, minimalist, or typographic archetype.
  - Cooking: slab or display serif. Warm, appetizing palette. Object or pattern archetype (ingredient motif).
  - Wellness: (inferred) light humanist sans or soft serif. Low-saturation naturals. Minimalist or organic-shape archetype.
  - Fiction: genre subtype drives the choice (thriller: condensed sans, dark plus red; romance: Didone serif, bright flat; fantasy: titling serif).
  - Kids: rounded sans. Vibrant palette. Illustrated or pattern archetype.
- **"Designed" vs "template-y" comes down to content-driven variation within a grid.** Penguin and Bickford-Smith keep a fixed grid but change the motif per book. For a procedural system, the theme should change the motif, palette, and title treatment, and different books should land on different archetypes. Having the theme only change text inside a fixed layout is exactly the "template with text swapped" failure.
- **Hard constraints a generator can enforce automatically:**
  - Max 2 families.
  - Title is the largest element.
  - Title auto-fits by switching to a condensed width or line-breaking before shrinking.
  - WCAG-style contrast check of text against its local background.
  - Text only in reserved "quiet zones" of the art.
  - Render a 160 px-wide preview and verify the minimum title cap-height (e.g., at least about 8–10 px at that size; this threshold is an inference, not sourced).
  - Add a 3–4 px gray border when the background luminance is very high (KDP rule).
- Output covers at a 1.6:1 portrait ratio, as PNG/JPEG at 1600×2560 for export. SVG can be used internally but must be rasterized for KDP.

### Gaps
- No authoritative genre conventions found for tech/how-to or wellness covers. The wellness and tech mappings above are inference.
- No empirical (A/B or eye-tracking) evidence on thumbnail legibility versus sales. The source is practitioner opinion, and some practitioners contest it.
- Cookbook and kids cover color/composition conventions are only covered generically (IngramSpark); the font lists are from one blog.
- Did not retrieve a quantitative title-size-to-cover-height ratio from any source.

## 2. Generative / procedural cover approaches

### Takeaway
The best-documented procedural cover system, NYPL Labs' 10PRINT-inspired generator, is fully deterministic. A seed derived from the title and author drives hue and complementary color, each title character maps to a geometric glyph tiled on a grid, and the text sits in a clean black-on-white block. Its author admits the covers "do not reflect the content of the book," which is exactly the gap this product must close by deriving the seed and motifs from the theme. Commercial tools such as Canva Magic Design work by matching prompts to a library of ready-made templates plus brand colors and fonts, not by generating layouts from scratch.

### Cited Findings
- **NYPL Labs Generative eBook Covers (Mauricio Giraldo Arteaga, 2014).** Built because NYPL's ebook app needed covers for public-domain books with missing or poor covers. — [NYPL blog](https://live-legacy-admin.nypl.org/blog/2014/09/03/generative-ebook-covers); [Mauricio Giraldo: Generative ebook covers](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Color: seed = `title.length + author.length`, mapped to a hue range of 30–260 (the range that "seemed to give the best results"). HSB color mode, brightness modulated by `counts % 20`. Secondary color is the complement (`(hue+180)%360`). — [Giraldo](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Shapes: each A–Z and 0–9 character maps to a PETSCII-derived geometric shape (e.g., Q = filled circle, W = open circle, A = triangle). Non-ASCII characters get a fallback mapping. Title characters are tiled on a grid, so the art is a "visual alphabet" of the title. — [Giraldo](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Scale: "the longer the title, the smaller the shape." Short titles produce big grids (e.g., a 3×3 grid for "Moby Dick"). — [Giraldo](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Type: Avenir Next, chosen for multilingual glyph coverage and consistency with the app UI. Text is always black on white for legibility. — [Giraldo](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Rejected predecessors: a generic image ("not a very elegant solution") and an iBooks-style cover ("a bit cheesy"). — [Giraldo](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Covers were rendered on-device rather than stored server-side. — [Giraldo](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Limitation, stated by the author: "Of course, these covers do not reflect the content of the book." — [Giraldo](https://mauriciogiraldo.com/blog/2014/10/10/generative-ebook-covers/)
  - Open-source port: `tenprintcover-py` on GitHub, used e.g. by the opacapp demo-covers repo. — [opacapp/demo-covers](https://github.com/opacapp/demo-covers)
  - The 10 PRINT inspiration: the Commodore 64 one-liner and the book led by Casey Reas, using the PETSCII character set. — [Nick Montfort: NYPL builds book covers from PETSCII](https://nickm.com/post/index.html%3Fp=3943.html)
- NYPL later ran "Recovering the Classics," inviting designers to make covers for public-domain classics that were left with "poorly designed or autogenerated covers." This is an implicit acknowledgment of the limits of generated covers. — [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2016/03/06/gorgeous-new-covers-for-100-gr.html); [American Libraries: Judging an ebook by its cover](https://americanlibrariesmagazine.org/blogs/e-content/judging-an-ebook-by-its-cover/)
- **Canva Magic Design:** the user enters a prompt or uploads media, and Magic Design returns up to 8 template-based designs that "adapt to your content." Brand Kit colors and fonts can be applied in one click. — [Canva Help: Use Magic Design](https://www.canva.com/help/use-magic-design/); [Canva: Magic Design](https://www.canva.com/magic-design/)
- **Designrr:** converts existing content (blogs, docs, audio/video) into ebooks via templates, and offers a 3D cover/mockup generator on Pro/Agency tiers. No evidence found that it generates covers from content semantics. — [Designrr help: 3D cover/mockup](https://learn.designrr.io/en/articles/646234-how-to-create-a-3d-cover-mock-up-pro-agency-premium-users-only)
- **Penguin series systems:** grid-constant, motif-variable designs (Marber grid; Bickford-Smith patterns conforming to one grid) are the human precedent for rule-based variety. — [Romek Marber](https://romekmarber.com/portfolio/penguin-grid); [Penguin](https://www.penguin.co.uk/discover/articles/coralie-bickford-smith-penguin-english-library)
- The Book Designer lists "pattern-based" covers (repeating abstract graphics) as a legitimate professional archetype when paired with strong typography. This validates SVG pattern generation as a non-amateur output. — [The Book Designer](https://www.thebookdesigner.com/types-of-book-covers-10-design-styles/)

### Inferences
- **A theme-aware procedural pipeline can extend the NYPL model:**
  1. The AI extracts genre, mood, and 3–5 concrete motif nouns from the outline.
  2. The genre selects the font pair, archetype set, and palette family (Section 1 map).
  3. The mood adjusts saturation and value. Motif nouns select icons or shapes from a curated SVG motif library, laid out by the archetype rules: single object (object archetype), tiled pattern (pattern/Bickford-Smith), or a large typographic title with a small accent glyph (typographic).
  4. A seeded RNG from the title gives deterministic variety (as NYPL did), so "regenerate" yields siblings rather than random noise.
  5. Constraint checks run last (contrast, thumbnail, quiet zones).
- Show 3–4 variants built from different archetypes, not color swaps of one layout. This mirrors Magic Design's multi-option output while avoiding its template-sameness.
- NYPL's "black on white text block" is a robust legibility fallback for any art-heavy composition.

### Gaps
- Did not find detailed public write-ups of Bauhaus/Swiss-style generative cover systems beyond the Penguin grids and NYPL. No sources were retrieved on Penguin's own generative or algorithmic work.
- Canva's internal method (how templates are ranked against a prompt) is undocumented publicly.
- No user research found on whether readers perceive pattern or abstract generated covers as "professional."

## 3. Shareability: formats, channels, share pages, creator pride

### Takeaway
Short eBooks are shared primarily as lead magnets (PDF delivered after email opt-in), as Gumroad products, and as LinkedIn document (PDF carousel) posts. Each channel has its own cover crop: portrait 1.6:1 for the book, 1280×720 landscape for Gumroad, about 1.91:1 (1200×630) for OG link previews, and the PDF first page as the LinkedIn carousel cover. A good share page uses a single column with a benefit headline, a 3D cover mockup, one clear CTA, and an OG image. Pride signals are mostly polish signals: a real cover with title and author, consistent branding, and professional interior typography.

### Cited Findings
- **Lead magnet pages:** a 3D cover mockup reportedly outperformed a flat cover image in an A/B test (vendor-reported, anecdotal). A "clean 3D mockup signals value." — [Apexure: ebook landing page](https://apexure.com/ebook-landing-page); [Thrive Themes](https://thrivethemes.com/smart-ebook-landing-page-templates/) (snippets)
- Recommended landing structure: single column, benefit-focused headline, 3D cover, and a one-click download button. A single clear CTA. Cover image plus benefit headline plus email capture plus social proof. — [SeedProd](https://www.seedprod.com/?p=38424); [Unbounce ebook landing page examples](https://unbounce.com/landing-pages/ebook-landing-page-examples/); [B12](https://www.b12.io/prompt-gallery/lead-magnet-landing-page/) (snippets)
- Lead-magnet PDF design: a simple cover stands out, and body text should be no smaller than 11 pt. — [Natsumi Design: Lead magnet PDF ebook](https://natsumidesign.squarespace.com/blog/lead-magnet-pdf-ebook) (snippet). Keep the design consistent with brand identity. — [Creative Market](https://creativemarket.com/blog/lead-magnet-templates) (snippet)
- Vendor opinion: a focused 10-page guide converts better than a sprawling 50-page ebook. Deliver the PDF immediately after opt-in. — [Inkfluence AI](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/ai-ebook-lead-magnet-without-designer-2026) (vendor claim, unverified)
- **Designrr** sells 3D cover/mockup generation as a premium feature, which suggests mockups are a valued deliverable for ebook creators. — [Designrr help](https://learn.designrr.io/en/articles/646234-how-to-create-a-3d-cover-mock-up-pro-agency-premium-users-only)
- **Gumroad:** cover image should be at least 1280×720 px at 72 DPI. — [Gumroad Help: Adding a cover image](https://gumroad.com/help/article/60-adding-a-cover-image). Third-party claims that the square thumbnail is auto-cropped from the cover center conflict with a Fiverr listing's 600×600 figure, so both are unconfirmed. — [topbubbleindex blog](https://www.topbubbleindex.com/blog/?p=5547)
- **LinkedIn document posts:** PDF, PPT, or DOC up to 300 pages and 100 MB. Suggested length is 5–15 slides. The document title displays above the carousel. Links inside the PDF are not clickable in the carousel. — [Hootsuite: LinkedIn carousel](https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-carousel); [Buffer: LinkedIn carousels](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-carousels/)
- Claimed 2.2–3.4× more reach for PDF carousels vs text/image posts (third-party algorithm study cited by a vendor). — [Agorapulse](https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/the-power-of-linkedin-pdf-carousels/) (vendor-cited, treat with caution)
- **OG images:** 1200×630 (about 1.91:1) is the de facto default. The Open Graph protocol doesn't mandate a size. Include `og:image:alt`. `twitter:image` overrides `og:image` on X. Keep key text away from edges. Verify the rendered card. — [HubSpot: Open Graph tags](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/open-graph-tags-facebook-twitter-linkedin); [Yoast: Open Graph](https://yoast.com/facebook-open-graph-protocol/) (Yoast suggests larger, 1200×800–2000×1600)
- **KDP-level polish requirements are also pride requirements:** title and author present and matching, no placeholder or template text, no blurred or cut-off text. — [Amazon KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G6GTK3T3NUHKLEFX)

### Inferences
- **"Finished book" screen should auto-produce a share kit from one cover source of truth:**
  - Portrait cover 1600×2560 PNG.
  - 3D/perspective mockup, which can be done in CSS 3D transforms without a backend.
  - 1200×630 OG/LinkedIn link card (cover mockup on the left plus title, subtitle, and author on a theme-colored field).
  - 1280×720 Gumroad banner.
  - PDF whose page 1 is the cover, so it works as a LinkedIn document post.
- **Read-online share page:** single column, mockup, title, subtitle, author byline, 1–2 sentence benefit blurb, "Read online" and "Download PDF" CTAs, and an optional table of contents preview. Without a backend, this can be simulated as a preview of the page plus an OG card preview showing how it unfurls on LinkedIn, X, and Slack.
- **Pride drivers to design for** (inferred from KDP requirements plus lead-magnet advice): the author's own name prominently on the cover and title page, an author bio and photo slot, no visible "template" artifacts, consistent theme across cover and interior, and a "looks like a real book" mockup moment at the end of the flow.
- Short-format bias: support 5–15 page outputs well, since that matches both lead-magnet and LinkedIn carousel norms.

- No primary research found on what makes creators "proud" to share (e.g., surveys of creators). Pride drivers above are inferred.
- No official LinkedIn spec retrieved for link-preview image size (1200×627 is commonly cited but was not verified at the source).
- Mockup-beats-flat-cover evidence is vendor-anecdotal only.
- Gumroad thumbnail behavior unconfirmed by official docs.

## 4. Interior typography basics for readable eBooks/PDF

### Takeaway
Butterick's rules give a ready-made spec:
- Body text 10–12 pt in print or 15–25 px on screen.
- Line spacing 120–145%.
- Line length 45–90 characters.
- Either first-line indents (1–4× point size) or 4–10 pt paragraph spacing, never both.
- Bold or italic sparingly and never together.
- All caps under one line, with 5–12% letterspacing.
- Hyphenation whenever text is justified.

Chapter openers conventionally use a 2–3 line drop cap followed by run-in small caps, applied identically to every chapter. Kindle and many e-readers render drop caps poorly, so PDF and web are safer targets.

### Cited Findings
- Point size 10–12 pt in print, 15–25 px on the web. Line spacing 120–145% of point size. Line length averages 45–90 characters. First-line indent 1–4× point size, or 4–10 pt between paragraphs, not both. Bold/italic as little as possible and never together. All caps only for less than one line, with 5–12% extra letterspacing for caps and small caps. Always hyphenate justified text. Keep kerning on. Avoid system fonts like Times New Roman and Arial. — [Butterick's Practical Typography: Summary of key rules](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html)
- Margins: on 8.5×11 at 12 pt, left and right margins of 1.5–2.0″ give a comfortable line length. Default 1″ margins make lines too long. Make the bottom margin about 0.25″ larger than the top to center the block visually. Butterick's example: 2″ margins, 11 pt, 15 pt leading reads better and can fit more words per page than 1″ margins with double spacing. — [Butterick: Page margins](https://practicaltypography.com/page-margins.html)
- Lead-magnet PDF body text should be at least 11 pt. — [Natsumi Design](https://natsumidesign.squarespace.com/blog/lead-magnet-pdf-ebook) (snippet)
- Drop caps: a 2-line drop cap reads modest or minimalist, 3-line reads traditional or literary, and 4+ lines tips into decorative. Follow the cap with run-in small caps so the eye doesn't jump from a huge letter to small text. Use the same treatment on every chapter, since mixing is the mistake. If a chapter opens with a quote mark, drop the quote and letter together or skip the cap. — [ebookpbook: Drop caps in self-published books](https://www.ebookpbook.com/2026/04/15/drop-caps-self-published-books/) (snippet)
- Many e-readers, particularly Kindle, do not support drop caps in ebook output. — [Pressbooks guide: Adding dropcaps](https://pressbooks.nebraska.edu/guide/chapter/adding-dropcaps-to-your-book/)
- The Book Designer documents 8 drop-cap styles for chapter openings, confirming drop caps as a standard chapter-opener device. — [The Book Designer: 8 drop caps for chapter openings](https://www.thebookdesigner.com/8-drop-caps-for-chapter-openings-in-adobe-indesign)

### Inferences
- **Interior tokens a generator can ship:**
  - Body 11 pt (PDF) or 18–20 px (read-online).
  - Line height 1.4.
  - Max width about 65ch.
  - Paragraph spacing OR indent, chosen per theme (indent for memoir and fiction feels bookish; spacing for business and how-to feels documentary). This genre mapping is inference.
  - Headings derived from the cover's display face for cover-to-interior continuity.
  - Body in a readable text face from the same pairing.
  - Chapter opener: chapter number plus title in the display face, extra top space (a "sink"), a 2- or 3-line drop cap by genre, and run-in small caps.
  - Pull quotes: display face, larger size, theme accent color, at most one per chapter (inference; no source found).
