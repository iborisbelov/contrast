Four ways to start a book on a phone

Today the first screen is a desktop layout folded into a column: an intro card, a progress bar, a heading, a textarea and a Continue button floating above a five-tab bar. It works, but it asks a first-timer to read a lot before typing, and it competes with the keyboard. Each concept below fixes that differently.

Sourdough for people with full‑time jobs

The input lives where the thumb and keyboard are: a chat-style composer pinned above the keyboard, with Continue merged into the send arrow. The bottom tab bar hides until the first answer (nothing to navigate yet). The intro shrinks to one line.

  • One element to understand, typing starts instantly
  • Never hidden by the keyboard
  • Feels like messaging, which everyone knows
  • Looks less “designed” than a full screen

Show the result before asking for input. Swipeable covers of finished books; tapping one opens it in the reader, then “Make one like this” pre-fills the questions. The text field stays for people who already know.

  • Answers “what will I get?” in one glance
  • Solves the blank page for novices
  • Examples can anchor people to the same topics
  • Needs 3–5 polished sample books

How should it sound?

“Most people think sourdough needs a free weekend. It doesn’t.”Warm & practical
“A starter is basically a low‑maintenance pet.”Playful

Replace the Editor/Book toggle with a mini book pinned at the top. Every answer lands on it (topic, reader, then a cover sketch, then the title), so progress is visible without switching views. Tap it to open the full book.

  • Keeps the “book grows as you answer” magic on phones
  • Removes a control (the toggle)
  • Cover is tiny until you tap
  • Takes a strip of vertical space

My pick: 1 + 4

The composer for the first answer (no reading, no hidden button), then the live book peek once there is something to show. Together they remove the intro card, the toggle and the tab bar from the first screen.

Keep from today

One question per screen, sample lines for the voice, “Skip, use your best guess”, and the sourdough example as a one-tap start.

How I’d test it

Five first-time users on their own phones, task “start a book about something you know”. Measure seconds to the first keystroke and how many open the book before the plan step.
